Once, before the sky was split into day and night, the Sun and the Moon lived together on Earth as inseparable companions.
The Sun was a creature of vibrant gold, overflowing with warmth and laughter. Wherever he walked, fields of wildflowers bloomed, and the world buzzed with joyful energy. The Moon was his quiet, silver counterpart. She was gentle and deep, moving with a calm grace that brought peace to everything she touched. Together, they kept the world in perfect harmony.
However, their combined radiance was overwhelming. With the Sun’s intense heat and the Moon’s brilliant silver light shining at the same time, the oceans began to dry up, and the creatures of the Earth grew too tired to sleep.
Seeing the exhaustion of the world, the Great Spirit of the Universe called them to the heavens. "You are both too magnificent to share the same stage," the Spirit told them. "To save the Earth, you must divide your duties."
The Sun, wanting to protect the living things he loved, volunteered to go first. He stepped into the great expanse, blazing with light to wake up the world and help the crops grow.
When he grew tired and dipped below the horizon, the Moon rose gracefully in his place. She softened the sky into an inky velvet black, cooling the scorched earth and wrapping the weary creatures in quiet shadows so they could finally rest and dream.
Though they were separated by an ocean of time, their bond never faded. The Sun left behind a trail of sunset colors—amber, pink, and violet—to welcome his friend to the sky. In return, the Moon gathered the discarded sparks of the Sun's brightest afternoons and scattered them across the dark as twinkling stars, a nightly reminder that he was never truly alone.
And on rare, magical days, the two are still granted a brief moment to meet. They rush into each other’s arms, a celestial eclipse—a brief, breathtaking reminder that even the greatest distances cannot break a true connection.
